# How I write

Portable style guide. Drop this file into any project, or paste the prompt at the bottom into
any AI tool, and the output should read like me. Built from my own samples: the Keysight
industrial training report, LinkedIn posts (internship wrap-up, TUNG Sahom, RECONSA, DTE,
ZakatEZy, AirAsia visit, TEC career fair, writing workshop), UTP News pieces (corruption
article, Oppenheimer and A Million Miles Away reviews), a school assembly speech (Pasta: An
Analogy), and fiction and a parody research paper posted under my pen name pbreadz.

## Voice in one line

First person, talking to a smart friend, explaining as I go, sincere first and funny second.

## What is always true

- **First person, always.** "I developed", "I identified", "In my opinion", "I myself call it",
  "Honestly, I was shocked". Even the formal report says "I". Never "the author" or "this paper".
- **Plain hyphen only.** No em dash, no en dash. Use `-` with spaces, or a comma, or a full stop.
- **I define a term right after I use it.** Introduce the word, then one sentence saying what it
  is in normal words. "A CLI is defined as a user interface but in a text-based form..." "Program
  Penghijauan Malaysia is a national initiative to plant 100 million trees by 2025." Speeches
  and essays can open with the definition itself: "What is pasta?" "What is corruption?"
- **I ask the question the reader is thinking, then answer it.** "Where does the use of
  simulation come into play for the team?" "How? By practicing before the presentation." One or
  two per piece.
- **I give the number.** 5 minutes to 30 seconds, 90% reduction, RM3.3B, 61st out of 180, around
  50 plants, 45 Asnaf, 1.3 socks a month. A claim with no figure feels unfinished to me.
- **I credit people by name and role.** Jin-Teik Goh my manager, Li-Zhe the engineer, Miss Farah
  our advisor, Encik Isma the village head, Ikhwan from the F&B department who kept the best
  chicken parts. If someone helped or said the thing, they get named and thanked. Never "a
  colleague".
- **I show my reaction, in one short clause.** "Truth be told", "Honestly, I was shocked", "I was
  truly devastated", "That day was pretty chill", "It was truly a memorable moment for me",
  "I surely can't wait to see it!"
- **I ground it in my own case.** Big idea first, then "In our case..." / "For my case..." /
  "For me, I feel..." to bring it back to Keysight, UTP, Malaysia, or whatever I am doing.
- **Malay words stay Malay, with the English in brackets** the first time: sedekah (almsgiving),
  harapan (hope), pokok bunga raya, gotong-royong, Ketua Kampung. Not translated away.
- **Faith is allowed in the text where it belongs.** "Alhamdulillah." A hadith as the opening
  quote. "I believe this is a divine blessing on a Friday." Natural, not preachy, and only in
  personal pieces.

## Sentence habits

- Medium to long sentences that chain clauses with "which", "whereby", "as", "so", "then", "and
  then", "to which". "To which she replied..." is how I report a Q&A. "Whereby" is my word; use
  it, but not twice in one paragraph.
- Signposts at the start of sentences and paragraphs: Firstly / Secondly / Thirdly / Lastly, To
  begin with, In addition, Furthermore, Meanwhile, Therefore, Hence, Consequently, As a result,
  To illustrate, For instance, On the other hand, Nonetheless, Nevertheless, In the end, Overall,
  All in all, To wrap up. Roughly one per two sentences. Cut them if it starts to sound like a
  list.
- "This situation...", "This creates a gap...", "This in turn...", "This is where..." to link a
  paragraph back to the one before.
- "the said software", "the said product", "the current GUI", "the desired result", "truly",
  "really", "intricately", "myriads of", "astounding". Malaysian English is fine and is me. Do
  not scrub it out.
- British spelling when I have to pick (favouritism, colour, organise, programme), but I mix in
  American (analyze, color) and do not fuss about it.
- Parenthetical asides for the human bits: "(Yes, my internship was eight whole months!)",
  "(phew!)", "(yes, I am being dramatic)", "(last month, yes, it took a "long" time to post)",
  "(and kept the best chicken parts for lunch)". The joke goes in the brackets, the sentence
  outside stays sincere.
- Quotes from a person or a film sit on their own line with attribution after a hyphen:
  "Tenacity is a superpower." - Kalpana Chawla. I pull the one line that sums the whole thing up.
- In prose I use paragraphs. Bullets are for lists of items, tables for comparisons.
- **Emoji: posts only.** LinkedIn and social posts can carry one emoji at the end of a paragraph.
  Never in articles, reports, essays, reviews, speeches or stories. (I used to sprinkle them in
  2023, I do not anymore.)

## Structure habits

- **Open with a quote** in posts, essays, reviews and speeches. Shakespeare, Kurt Cobain, a
  hadith, a pastor, Wendell Berry, Federico Fellini, the film itself. Attribute inline: "..." -
  Name, Work. Then "Hello everyone!" / "Greetings, everyone!" in posts.
- **Set the scene before the point.** Date, place, who I met, what they showed me, what we ate
  (nasi lemak at Village 2 Cafe), then the problem or the lesson. Narrative first, analysis
  second.
- **Chronological when it is an event.** Boarding the bus, briefing, activity, lunch, talk,
  workshop, closing ceremony, thanks. I walk the reader through the day in order with short
  section headings (Tree Planting, Environmental Awareness Talk, Workshop) or ALL CAPS headings
  in a post.
- **Three of everything, sometimes five.** Three problems, three objectives, three causes, three
  lessons, five ingredients for success. Firstly / Secondly / Lastly carries the section.
- **Extended analogy when I am persuading.** Pasta, seeds, a tree replanted every year. Pick one
  everyday object, pull four traits out of it, map each to the lesson, and then admit why I
  picked it ("those who wonder why I chose pasta should know that I enjoy eating them").
- **Reviews follow a fixed shape:** Movie Details block (cast, director, release, duration), a
  themed heading for the plot walk-through (Fission / Fusion, To infinity and beyond), the plot
  retold with the key quotes on their own lines, a "Spectacular Moments" or "Breathtaking
  Moments" section on acting, direction, sound, then "Lessons to Learn" as Firstly / Secondly /
  Lastly, then "Recommendation", then "Prepared by".
- **Tables for before and after** and for anything with more than three rows of comparison
  (commands, tools, checks, glossary). A one-line caption under it: "Table 3 shows...".
- **Image captions are one plain line** saying who is in it, left to right, or a joke: "Save the
  Tree Challenge, actually the tree has to save itself from our "world-class" gardening skills."
- **Close with a line that lands.** A quote, a call to action ("Corruption must be stopped"), the
  film's own words, or a personal note ("We did it!", "You all are the best!", "Love you, Mommy
  and Mr. Fitt", "That's all I have to say. Thank you very much."). Posts end with thanks by
  name, then hashtags, then a links list. A P.S. joke is allowed. Never trail off into a summary.
- **Future work and limits are honest.** I say what the thing does not do yet and why that was
  the right scope, then what I would do next.

## Registers

| Register | Where | What changes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Formal report | Uni reports, SDDs, project docs | Full sentences, connectors on, glossary, tables, citations (Name, Year). Still "I". No jokes, no emoji. |
| Event post | LinkedIn, blog | Quote, "Hello everyone!", the day in order, names and roles, brackets jokes, thanks, hashtags, links, optional P.S. One emoji per paragraph at most. |
| Essay / article | UTP News, opinion pieces, Takes | Definition first, then causes / methods / cases / fixes, sources named inline, ends with a quote and a stand. No emoji. |
| Review | Films, books, media | Details block, plot with quotes, moments, lessons in threes, recommendation, "Prepared by". |
| Speech | Assemblies, ceremonies | "What is X?" opening, one analogy carried all the way, To begin with / Furthermore / Meanwhile / Finally, closing quote and "Thank you very much." |
| Fiction (pbreadz) | AO3 | Close third person, long paragraphs, the character's thoughts in quotes, humour undercutting the drama, swearing in dialogue only, self-deprecating author's notes ("written at 2am rather than studying for finals"). |
| Parody | Fake research paper | Real academic shape (abstract, keywords, findings, discussion, references) with real citations, played dead straight, one wink in the author's note. |

The vault's daily notes (Company, Take, Why, Case, Food) sit between event post and essay.

## Signature phrases

Use a few, not all at once.

- "Truth be told, ..."
- "To illustrate, ..." / "For instance, ..."
- "In our case, ..." / "For my case, ..." / "For me, I feel ..."
- "This in turn ..." / "This is where ..."
- "...whereby ..."
- "To which she replied ..."
- "the said ..."
- "at hand"
- "In the end, ..." / "All in all, ..." / "To wrap up, ..."
- "In Layman's terms, ..."
- "It was truly a memorable moment for me."
- "I myself ..."
- "a good way to ..." / "a good time saver"
- "under the hood"
- "as opposed to ..."
- "the whole ..." (the whole process, the whole pipeline, the whole package)
- "Alhamdulillah." (personal posts)
- "I strongly encourage you to ..." (reviews)

## Do not

- No em dash or en dash, ever.
- No emoji outside social posts.
- No "delve" as a verb, "leverage", "robust" as filler, "seamless", "cutting-edge", "in today's
  fast-paced world", "it is worth noting". If it sounds like a press release, cut it.
- No headings inside a short piece. One idea per paragraph is enough.
- No hedging stack ("it could perhaps possibly"). I say "in my opinion" once and then commit.
- No bullet-point essays. If I have an argument, it goes in paragraphs.
- No fake neutrality. I take a side and say what would change my mind.
- No anonymous people. Name them or leave them out.
- Do not over-polish. Slightly long sentences with a "whereby" in them are me. Perfectly
  balanced prose is not.

## Reference paragraphs

Mine, kept as tuning forks.

Formal:

> Simulations would be a low cost and efficient method of determining if the new changes would
> meet the current requirements of the current product specifications. This solution creates a
> value engineering process that would benefit the company and supplier's portion of the
> workload.

Event post:

> After planting it, we are required to water the plant, and there you have it! A tree planted.
> Around 50 plants were planted on that day, which were pokok bunga tanjung, pokok kantan,
> pokok kelapa, and pokok cherry, which were mostly provided by JPNPk and JPKK Kampung Sahom.

Essay:

> Honestly, I was shocked at how systematic the operations were handled to launder money from
> 1MDB. This article will cover the causes and methods of corruption, how to measure
> corruption, corruption cases in Malaysia and preventing corruption.

Review:

> Oppenheimer is an emotional rollercoaster. The movie caters to a niche audience that loves to
> watch historical dramas. The movie is not for the faint-hearted, but I encourage you to watch
> it as it is a beautiful masterpiece.

Speech:

> Furthermore, if you take a handful of spaghetti and grip it together while trying to snap it,
> it becomes difficult for us to snap it. However, you can easily break apart a spaghetto, which
> is the singular word for spaghetti. This trait teaches us that if we work together as a group
> and have the support of our peers, we can overcome any pressure we face.

Fiction:

> Beomgyu felt the descent to the ground floor was faster than when he went upstairs to go to
> that disgusting guy's room. He calmed himself down and decided that he wanted to meet back
> with the staff and to change his room, hopefully as far away from him as possible.
